# Content templates and reference

## Structure by content type

**Blog post** — headline (≤60 chars, keyword) → intro (100-150w, hook + what it covers) → 3-5 sections with H2 subheadings, one idea each → conclusion (75-100w) with CTA → meta description (≤160 chars).

**LinkedIn post** — hook line → 2-4 short paragraphs or a personal-story arc → one CTA (comment / click / share) → 3-5 hashtags. ~1,300 characters is the sweet spot before "see more."

**X/Twitter** — punchy opening, thread for longer narratives.

**Instagram** — visual-first caption, storytelling hook, line breaks, hashtags in first comment or at end.

**Newsletter** — subject line (<50 chars, 2-3 options) → preview text (complements, doesn't repeat subject) → header/value line → 2-3 scannable sections → one primary CTA button → sign-off → unsubscribe note (RGPD requirement).

**Landing page** — headline (<10 words, benefit-first) → subheadline → hero (headline+CTA+visual) → 3-4 value props → social proof → objection handling/FAQ → final CTA repeated.

**Press release** — factual headline (<80 chars) → dateline → lead paragraph (who/what/when/where/why) → body with quotes → boilerplate → media contact.

**Case study** — title ("[Customer] achieves [result] with [product]") → snapshot (industry, size, result) → challenge → solution → results with numbers → quote → CTA.

**SaaS launch announcement** — what shipped, who it's for, the one problem it solves, how to try it (link/CTA), what's next.

## Headline / hook formulas

- "How to [result] [without common obstacle]"
- "[Number] [adjective] ways to [result]"
- "Why [common belief] is wrong (and what to do instead)"
- Hooks: surprising stat, contrarian statement, direct question, relatable scenario, bold claim.

## CTA principles

Action verbs (Get, Start, Try, See). Specific over vague ("Start your free trial" > "Submit"). Reduce risk when true ("No card required", "Cancel anytime"). One primary CTA per piece.

## Campaign brief structure (launches)

1. Overview — name, one-sentence summary, SMART objective
2. Audience — segment, pain points, buying stage, where they spend time
3. Key messages — one core message + 3-4 supporting points with proof
4. Channel strategy — owned (blog/email/social/webinar) / earned (PR/guest/community/reviews) / paid (search/social ads/events); why each fits, effort level
5. Content calendar — table: Week | Piece | Channel | Owner | Status; note dependencies (e.g. "landing page live before ads launch")
6. Content pieces needed — asset, description, priority (must-have/nice-to-have)
7. Success metrics — primary KPI + target, 3-5 secondary KPIs, tracking method
8. Budget (if given) — split by channel, 10-15% contingency
9. Risks — 2-3, each with a mitigation
10. Next steps — immediate actions, approvals needed

## Small-team budget/channel benchmarks (indicative, EUR)

- Marketing spend: 5-15% of revenue is typical for B2B SaaS; early-stage/growth often 15-25%.
- A common starting split: 30-40% paid acquisition, 20-30% content production, 10-20% events, 10-15% tools, 5-10% testing. Adjust freely for a bootstrapped team — content and organic usually dominate pre-Series A.
- Production time: blog post 0.5-1 day, newsletter 0.5 day, social post 1-2h, landing page 1-2 days, short video 2-5 days — scale down for a solo founder using AI drafting.

## Cadence guidance for a small team

Blog: 1-2x/week max realistic for 1 person. Newsletter: bi-weekly is sustainable. Social: 3-5 posts/week on one or two channels beats a thin spread across five. Leave slack — a stalled calendar is worse than a slower one.
