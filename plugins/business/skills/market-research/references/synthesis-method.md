# Research synthesis method

## Thematic analysis
1. Familiarize — read everything before coding anything.
2. Initial coding — tag each observation/quote with a descriptive code; be generous, merge later.
3. Theme development — group related codes into candidate themes.
4. Theme review — check against the data: enough evidence, themes distinct, coherent story.
5. Refine — name each theme, write a 1-2 sentence description.
6. Report as findings with supporting evidence.

## Affinity mapping (alternative / collaborative)
One observation per note, cluster by similarity without pre-defined categories, label clusters, look for
higher-level groupings. Move notes freely — the first grouping is rarely best. Outliers are interesting, don't
force them into a cluster.

## Triangulation
- **Methodological** — same question, different methods (interview + survey + analytics).
- **Source** — same method, different participants/segments.
- **Temporal** — same observation at different times.
A finding backed by multiple sources/methods is much stronger than one source alone.

## Qual vs quant
Qualitative reveals what and why (generates hypotheses); quantitative reveals how much and how many (tests
them at scale). When they disagree, that's signal — check if different populations were measured, or if stated
preference (survey) differs from actual behavior (analytics). Report disagreement honestly rather than picking
a side.

## Survey pitfalls to avoid
Reporting averages without distributions (a 3.5 average can mean uniform lukewarm or a 50/50 love/hate split);
ignoring non-response bias; over-interpreting small differences (0.1 NPS point is noise); treating Likert scale
gaps as equal intervals.

## Opportunity sizing
Estimate addressable users, frequency, severity, and willingness to pay. Show the math and use ranges, not
false precision ("1,500-2,500 users/month", not "2,137"). Score = impact (users x frequency x severity) x
evidence strength x strategic alignment x feasibility. Be transparent about assumptions.

## Common mistakes
Demographic personas instead of behavioral ones; more than 5 personas; forcing a predetermined narrative;
treating a quote as a finding rather than evidence for one; recommendations too vague to act on ("improve
onboarding" vs "add a progress indicator to the setup flow").
